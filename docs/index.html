Why it exists

Most teams lose in the first two hours.

They lose before writing a line of code. Hack Kit turns those two hours into a guided path.

01

The cool problem

Teams pick what sounds exciting, not what the rubric rewards. Hack Kit scores statements on the real rubric weights.

02

The imaginary user

They forget the elderly person with a keypad phone. Every Hack Kit command thinks as the least-digital user.

03

The first idea

Most teams build the first idea they think of. /hack:ideate explores six genuinely different directions before anyone commits.

04

Guesses as facts

Nobody tests the belief the idea rests on. Every claim gets a label, and /hack:assumptions finds what to test first.

⚖️

Hack Kit judges whether the problem is real and whether your solution truly solves it for real users. It never judges business viability, revenue or market size.

Hack Court 2.0 · inside /hack:pick, /hack:verify and /hack:court

Twelve jurors. One question.

"Does this solve a real problem for its real users?" Each juror is a separate subagent who can't see the others' votes.

  1. Evidence pack. Every claim labelled: verified, team-provided, AI inference or unknown.
  2. Prosecution. The strongest case that it does not solve a real problem.
  3. Defense. The strongest case that it does.
  4. Jury. 12 jurors vote GUILTY, NOT GUILTY or INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE, each with a weakest assumption and a rated fix.
  5. Judge. PASS, REVISE, FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE, with ranked improvements and the next experiment. Then /hack:improve.
0 NOT GUILTY · 0 GUILTY · 0 INSUFFICIENT

Demo with sample votes for a sample idea ("voice check-in for hospital OPD patients"), using the healthcare domain seats. The real trial runs inside Claude Code. Jurors are simulated perspectives, and confidence isn't a probability of success.

What you get

One folder. One truth.

Everything lives in .hackathon/ inside your project. Commit it, and the whole team works from the same state.

  • Stop and resume any time. Each command reads STATE.md first.
  • Each output file comes from a template with the same name.
  • Every claim is labelled [V] verified, [T] team-provided, [AI] inference or [?] unknown. Only real evidence can validate an assumption.
  • Nothing is lost: earlier versions go to history/, and rejected ideas are logged with the reason.
  • /hack:ship compiles it all into FINAL.pdf, with a cover, contents and page numbers.

Questions

Frequently asked.

Will it pick the idea for us?

No. /hack:ideate shows the trade-offs and the captain decides. Every big choice is a question to your team.

Can we trust the verdict?

Use it to decide what to test and fix, not as proof. Jurors are simulated perspectives, confidence isn't a probability, and only verified or team-provided evidence counts as real. Real validation comes from the experiments you run.

How much usage does a trial cost?

A full trial uses about 15 subagent calls: 2 lawyers, 12 jurors and any retries. Quick mode (--quick) uses about 9. Each /hack:improve round runs one quick re-trial.

Does it need web access?

The research steps (decode, validate, compete) work best with web search. Without it, Hack Kit still runs, but marks claims UNVERIFIED and tells you what to look up.

What do I need for the PDF?

Python 3, plus Chrome/Chromium, Playwright, or pandoc with a PDF engine. If none of these is available, open FINAL.html and use Print → Save as PDF.

Why no business or market judging?

At the idea stage, judges reward a real problem that's clearly solved. If your rubric asks for business points, /hack:pitch covers them.

Is it free?

Yes. It's open source under the MIT License. You need your own Claude access to run it.
